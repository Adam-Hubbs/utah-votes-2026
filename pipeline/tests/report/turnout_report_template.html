Utah 2026 election site / expected-vote model

How good is our turnout model?

An out-of-sample check of the two parts of model_expected.R: the pre-election turnout prior and the live estimate of eventual ballots, using vote counts recovered from archived Utah results pages for 2016 to 2025. Prepared 23 Oct 2026, eleven days before the election.

Verdict on the model as it stood on 23 Oct (before the changes below)

  • The pace curve is the main problem, and it is too fast. Real Utah counts reached about 70% of the final total by 22 to 29 hours after close in 2018, 2020 and 2022. The deployed curve says 84% at 22 hours. Every early observation sits below the model's median. Full ballots were not in until about day 9.
  • Live accuracy is only adequate, and the intervals are too narrow where it matters. Replaying real county completion patterns, the statewide median of expected ballots runs high (mean +12% at 24 hours) and the 90% statewide interval covers about 7 in 10 cases, not 9. At county level, small counties are covered only 4 in 10 times at 24 hours because they finish earlier than the model assumes.
  • The pre-election prior is reasonable for what it can know. The statewide 90% interval held the truth in 5 of 6 back-tests, but it is wide (34% to 71% of the total), and the point estimate missed by 4% to 40%. The county pattern given the right statewide level is good (weighted error 1.5% to 9%).
  • Data answer: county-by-time counts do exist for 2022 (one full 29-county vector at +22 h plus statewide points from +12 h), 2020 (+97 h onward), 2016 and 2018, and a single post-HB 300 point from 2025. Nothing usable survives for 2024. That is thin, so the conclusions about pace rest on about ten statewide points from four elections.

Changes adopted (branch turnout-fix)

Everything in sections 2 to 5 describes the old model, so the findings stay checkable. The recalibration below was chosen from the replays in this report. It prefers settings that give honest, wide intervals over the lowest error, because two county vectors are too few to tune on.

  • Pace curve. tau_hours 22 to 70, m_inf 0.975 to 0.99, m0 stays 0.62. Results hardly moved between tau 50 and 95, so 70 sits between the 2025-style and 2016 to 2022 fits.
  • County size. New pace_size_slope = 0.6: the logit of a county's counted share falls 0.6 per unit of log registration, centred on the registration-weighted mean. Captures gave 0.3 to 0.8.
  • Dispersion. Beta concentration k 12 to 6 (archived dispersion implies 3 to 14).
  • Wider uncertainty. Statewide pace scale 0.7/0.5 to 0.9/0.6, county turnout noise 0.17/0.6 to 0.22/0.7, county likelihood power 0.35 to 0.25. Without this the county 90% intervals still covered only 68% to 82% of mid and large counties at 24 hours.
  • Turnout shift prior left alone (mu_delta 0.10, sigma_delta 0.6). The base year is already 2022 (turnout_2022), so 2026 is a midterm-to-midterm step, which is the one thing the +40% miss (2022 from 2020, presidential to midterm) does not describe. No pair in the archive is midterm to midterm within one roll definition, so there is no evidence to move it; the wide t3 prior held the truth in 5 of 6 pairs.
  • Front end. Until 36 hours after close, or while the statewide 90% range is wider than 25 points, the site shows "about 55–80%" with a tooltip instead of a single percent.

1. Do we have county-by-time data?

Yes, for the elections before 2023 and one in 2025, but only as sparse Wayback Machine captures, not complete logs. The old Lt. Governor site (electionresults.utah.gov/elections/countyCount/<contest>, used through 2022) published a table of every county's votes for each contest. The Internet Archive crawled those pages at irregular times. We parsed every capture into pipeline/data/timing/county_count_timeline.csv (built by pipeline/scripts/build_count_timeline.R).

What did not work

Clarity ENR (results.enr.clarityelections.com/UT/...) returns 403 for every path now, and the Wayback Machine holds it only for Salt Lake County (2019 to 2022, three 2022 versions). The new results site has no early captures for 2024: its turnout and stats endpoints are empty and the general-election export was first captured on 17 Nov 2024. The county API pages for 2024 were crawled on 19 Nov or later. The 2025 export has three captures (one before close, one at +19 h, one final) and covers municipal contests only, so counts there are sums of votes across all contests in a county. A "County Completion Report" page, which shows when each county last released results, was also captured and parsed.

2. Pace of the count

The model assumes that on average a fraction m(h) = 0.975 − 0.355 e−h/22 of eventual ballots is counted h hours after the 8 pm Mountain close. Figure 1 compares that with the share of the final top-of-ticket total in every archived statewide capture.

3. The pre-election turnout prior

The prior sets eventual ballots in county c to Rc × expit(logit(turnoutprev,c) + δ + εc) with δ ~ 0.10 + 0.6 t3. To test it, each election year is predicted from an earlier one with the unmodified model code, with no counts observed. Truth is top-of-ticket votes (President or Senate). County registration is known only for 2022 and 2026, so other years scale the statewide active-voter total by a county share extrapolated from the 2022 to 2026 trend. Active-roll definitions changed between 2018 and 2020 (statewide active voters rose from 1.39 million to 1.68 million), so pairs that cross that line, and any pair where the election type changes, are poor analogues for 2026-from-2022. They are shown for completeness.

4. Live estimate of eventual ballots

4a. Replays of real county vectors

We have two usable full county vectors: the 2022 Senate race at +22.2 h (70% counted) and the 2020 presidential race at +97 h (87% counted). Each is pushed through fit_expected_ballots() at its real timestamp, with a prior from an earlier cycle, once as deployed (mu_delta = 0.10) and once with the prior centred on the true statewide shift to isolate the live component.

4b. Warped real patterns across hours since close

To see accuracy by time, a real completion pattern (2022 at +22 h, 2020 at +97 h, 2025 at +19 h, with its county-by-county gradient) is shifted in logit space until the statewide weighted share equals the empirical curve from Figure 1, then applied to other years' true county totals. This yields 10 combinations at each of eight hours. It uses real county heterogeneity but a pace curve fitted to the same points, so it favours a recalibrated model somewhat; read the "after" numbers as optimistic by that amount. "Before" is the model as of 23 Oct, "after" the recalibrated defaults.

4c. Comparison with the synthetic back-test

The existing back-test (backtest_results.csv) reports county 90% coverage of 0.91 to 0.99 and statewide coverage of 0.88 to 1.00 at 1, 14 and 36 hours. It generates fake nights with a uniform statewide pace of 50% to 85% at the first drop and a geometric catch-up, and gives the model a prior within ±0.2 logit of the truth. Real nights differ in three ways that matter: the count is slower than that process after the first day (about 72% to 76% at 29 to 36 hours, not 93%), small counties finish far ahead of large ones, and the priors in this evaluation missed the statewide level by more than 0.2 logit in every pair. The synthetic coverage figures should be read as an upper bound.

5. Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • The heavy-tailed statewide shift is justified. Realised shifts ranged from −1.5 to +1.0 logit across pairs, and 5 of 6 statewide 90% intervals held the truth.
  • Given the right statewide level, county ordering is good: weighted error 1.5% to 9%, within a factor of the model's county noise term (standardised residual sd 0.7 to 1.4).
  • By +97 h the live model is accurate (2020 replay: statewide error about 1.5%, county coverage 97%).
  • The code guarantees counted ≤ T and degrades to the prior when nothing is counted.

Weaknesses

  • Pace curve: too fast by 10 to 15 points between 12 and 100 hours (Figure 1).
  • Exchangeable counties: small counties finish early and large ones late, so the Beta draws are mis-specified, and county intervals under-cover (Figure 5).
  • Early statewide level is not identified. At +6 h, a 20% to 30% error in the turnout prior survives into the posterior.
  • County concentration k = 12 is higher than the dispersion in the data implies (implied k of 3 to 14, median about 5).
  • Evidence base is small: two county vectors, about ten statewide points, one post-HB 300 observation. Counts after HB 300 (ballots must arrive by 8 pm Election Day) may be faster than 2016 to 2022; 2025 at +19 h was 74.8%, versus 70% at +22 h in 2022.

6. Recommendations (status after the change)

Items 1 to 3 and the wider-intervals part of 4 were adopted in the recalibration at the top; 5 to 7 remain open.

  1. Slow the statewide pace curve. Keep m0 = 0.62, raise m_inf to 0.99 and set tau_hours between 60 (consistent with the 2025 point) and 90 (2016 to 2022 fit; unrestricted fit 97 h). Alone this does not reduce replay bias (Figure 4, "slower pace"), because the exchangeable-county structure is the larger problem, so ship it together with recommendation 2.
  2. Make pace depend on county size. Use logit mc = logit m(h) + η − γ log(Rc/10,000) with γ about 0.3 to 0.75 (Figure 2 slopes). This needs a code change, which was out of scope here; the replay harness in pipeline/tests/eval_turnout_model.R will score it.
  3. Lower the Beta concentration to about 6. It cut early statewide MAPE from 16% to 12.5% at 3 hours and from 14% to 9% with a correctly centred prior (Figure 4, third line), and moves county coverage towards nominal.
  4. Do not present early percent-of-expected as precise. Before about 36 hours, show the interval or a range and say that the estimate is prior-driven. Statewide 90% coverage before 24 hours was 50% to 60% in the replays.
  5. Use any ballots-remaining statement. remaining_ballots.csv removes the turnout/pace ambiguity and the evidence shows no other input does.
  6. Log the 2026 night. The scraper already stores a timeline; keep every version, then refit m(h) and the size gradient on the first post-HB 300 midterm, which will be the best evidence available.
  7. Leave the turnout prior roughly as is. Do not narrow sigma_delta; realised shifts were as large as the prior allows. Consider a wider county term only if size-dependent pace is not adopted.

7. Methods and limitations

  • Truth. Top-of-ticket votes: OpenElections (2016 precinct file, 2018 county file), Wayback captures of the official county table taken weeks after close (2020, 2022), and the project's county_baselines.csv (2024). Votes stand in for ballots; the shortfall (about 1%) is the same in prior and truth.
  • Registration. County registration exists in the repo for 2022 and 2026 only. Earlier and 2024 values are the statewide active total from Utah's weekly registration series times an extrapolated county share, so county prior errors for those years include share-drift error.
  • Capture times. Wayback times are when the page was crawled, not when results were published; the site refreshed every 60 seconds. Utah counties usually post once or twice a day after election night, so the staircase shape of the count is real but its timing is approximate.
  • Partial finals. Final totals for 2018 and 2020 are from OpenElections and the last archived capture (about 10+ days after close). The 2016 point at +43 h used a county table whose final capture was 11 days after close.
  • Salt Lake Clarity series stops at 263,191 ballots on 11 Nov 2022 although canvass was about 381,000, which independently shows that Salt Lake had counted only 69% at +66 h.
  • Small samples. Replays reuse the same few patterns, so the 10 combinations per cell are not independent; read coverage figures as indicative, with a standard error of at least 0.15.
  • Not tested. Contest-level propagation (pct_expected for individual races), the needle model, and the effect of ballots_returned.csv.
